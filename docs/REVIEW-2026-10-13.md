**How the review was done.** Eight web-research sweeps (about 150 primary sources read: trick-taking AI state of the art; multi-sided search and solver feasibility; whole-match objectives; belief and inference; self-play learning; the Forty-fives family and other 41 software; engineering and version checks; evaluation statistics), then five reviewer lenses over the plan (objective, solver and search, training, engineering and operations, rules), a merge into 57 distinct proposed changes, three independent sceptics on each of the top 45 (44 survived, usually with amendments; 1 rejected), a synthesis report (part 1), a completeness critic (part 2), a loose-ends sweep that read the key papers directly and corrected two earlier claims (part 3), and a rules cross-check against the only other documented "41", the Irish 25 app (part 4). Nothing in the repository was changed by the review itself.

**Owner decisions taken during the review.**
- Stakes: every loser pays the same (H6 as written); the four-way money-chance type stays.
- Burning: no ace of any suit may ever be burned, by the dealer or the robber (RULES.md H3 amended). The dealer must take a turned Joker; the ace-of-trumps holder must rob at their own turn in trick 1 (H2/H4 unchanged).
- Thinking time: up to 8 s on trick 1 and 5 s on trick 2, the same on phone and server (tricks 3-4 assumed at most 3 s; trick 5 is forced).
- An early playable "measurement build" is wanted (checkpoint M3b).
- Tournament mixes: equal weight on all 14 candidate/incumbent mixes.
- 41 is the same family as the Irish 25 app's "41"; the house rules stand; a `bonus_later` rule switch (5 house / 6 flat-11) is added to the engine.

**Two corrections to the research made by the loose-ends sweep.** The plan's Sturtevant figures (Hearts 8.01 vs 8.99, Spades 5.72 vs 5.61) are exactly right (IJCAI 2003 Table 12), so the first sweep's objection is withdrawn; and the claim that plain reinforcement learning "failed" in the Nova Scotia 45s project is not documented in that repository and is dropped. Where parts 1 and 3 disagree on a number, part 3 (primary source read directly) wins.

**Reviewer-reported Cloudflare figures conflict** (script size limit, startup limit, Durable Object attachment size); the plan designs for the stricter values and re-verifies them against live documentation at M3b.

# Part 1: synthesis report

## Final review of `docs/PLAN.md` for the 41 bots

*Prepared for the project owner, 13 October 2026. Technical terms are explained in [square brackets] the first time they appear. This review draws on eight research sweeps (about 150 primary sources read), five reviewer lenses, and a three-sceptic vote on every proposed change. Nothing in the repository was altered.*

---

### 1. Verdict

Yes, this is the right plan: its recipe (a fast exact solver for the "all cards face up" game, sampled guesses of the hidden cards weighted by inference, one level of recursive rollouts with realistic simulated players, a learned shared blueprint, and everything scored in one four-way "money chance" type and promoted only through duplicate tournaments) is still the proven winning recipe for trick-taking games, and nothing published between 2019 and 2026 overturns it. Three load-bearing assumptions must change before the plan can be executed as a schedule: a full open-hand solve of one guessed deal is **not** cheap at the start of a hand (seconds to minutes per guess, not milliseconds), so the "tens of millions of exact teacher hands" and "thousands of worlds" budgets are unaffordable and unsupported; the match-equity model can and should be an **exact table** rather than a learned guess, built at M1 so the M2 solver has something to score with; and the parts of the learning pipeline that decide whether learning works (the per-trick reward, the solver's tie rule, the belief model's form, the league, network sizes and throughput) are under-specified in ways that would let the single money objective drift in implementation. Everything else is specification and engineering housekeeping that keeps the plan's methods and makes "one objective everywhere" provable rather than asserted; 43 of 44 proposed changes survived the sceptics (usually with amendments, all incorporated below), and the one rejected change is recorded in section 6.

### 2. What the plan gets right and must not change

- **One objective, one type (PLAN 5.3, CLAUDE.md, RULES H6–H7).** Expected money = 40 × (chance of winning the match) − 10 under H6, so maximising money is exactly maximising match-win chance; the four chances sum to 1; the compiler-checked money-chance type is the only score the solver, search, training targets and tournaments can produce. The only trick-taking evidence on this question (Sturtevant 2004: Hearts to 100 points, Spades to 200) shows a game-level objective beats a hand-points objective (54.5% game wins despite a worse average score). Keep every word of it, including "a bot that wins more games but less money is worse".
- **Per-trick re-evaluation of the money (5.3, 6.6, 6.8, 8).** Points land the moment a trick is won and a game can end mid-hand, so "public enemy number one" must be re-assessed after every trick. Right in principle and in each place the plan applies it.
- **max^n rather than paranoid as the decision rule (6.7, 16).** [max^n: every side picks what is best for itself; paranoid: assume everyone gangs up on you.] Sturtevant's thesis shows paranoid gains search depth but no playing strength in card games, and the closest same-family project (an open-source Nova Scotia 45s engine, 2026) found a paranoid oracle "about half the true ceiling". Blocking the leader must emerge from the money values, not from a hard-coded coalition rule.
- **Recursive rollouts as the main engine (6.8).** [Rollout: play the candidate card, then simulate the rest of the hand with players who see only their own cards.] Confirmed independently in Skat (Furtak & Buro 2013: about +7 points per game over saturated PIMC) and in bridge at exactly our five-card hand size (Bouzy, Rimbaud & Ventos 2020: +0.24 tricks per deal). It is the only published engine that values concealment, reneging and hidden burns correctly. Keep common random numbers, adaptive sampling, the deviation threshold and the alternatives pool.
- **Hard-fact tracker, constraint-respecting world generator, policy-based inference, particle reuse, weight floor (6.3–6.4).** All verified precedents (GIB; Kermit's inference worth 996 vs 779 points per 36 games; Rebstock 2019 +2.32/+0.64/+1.57 points per game; History Filtering 2023). The floor turns out to be a correctness requirement for the sampler, not just robustness.
- **Match-equity modelling on backgammon's tables and Suphx (6.6).** Right precedents, right inputs (with two simplifications in section 4).
- **The oracle value head used in training only (6.5, 8).** PerfectDou's own ablation shows plain PPO without it is weak; the all-cards-visible judge is where its ten-fold sample advantage comes from. Keep it, and keep it out of real-game decisions (with the one clarification in section 3.4).
- **PIMC teacher → self-play RL with PPO (DMC as challenger) → Expert Iteration → students → human compatibility, every promotion gated by duplicate tournaments and SPRT (8, 9).** The closest four-player partnership precedents (PerfectDou, DanZero+, Ataraxos) use the same pieces.
- **Partner-coordination safeguards (6.10) and phase E ordering (human models only predict; the bot still maximises money; piKL at search time).** Exactly the SPARTA, Cicero and piKL-SPARTA recipe.
- **Integer-only deterministic engine, own pinned RNG, golden vectors on three platforms, action-only replays, independent naive reference, 10-million-game differential gate, one encoder in Rust (6.1, 6.5, 13, 14).** This is what makes every later gate testable.
- **House rules H1–H7 and the interpretation notes.** H1 (illegal plays impossible) removes turned partnerships from the engine, solver, belief and records, and keeps every trick an A,B,C,D,A,B,C,D cycle. The reneging test, plain-suit legality, trump bookkeeping and bonus bookkeeping are exact renderings of rules 6–31. The end-check order gives an unambiguous mid-hand game end, and the bonus flag is provably equal to (score mod 5 == 1).
- **Tech stack (11).** Every one of the ~35 pinned versions is correct as of today; the "stay behind" choices (Python 3.14, PyTorch 2.14.1+cu126) are justified by primary sources. Run operations (seeded chunks, R2 as the single truth, destroy-never-stop, explicit thread counts, self-destruction with the instance's own key) are sound.
- **What it deliberately does not do (16):** no CFR, no endgame database, no hand-written rules engine, no paranoid, no plain PIMC as the final engine, no cluster frameworks, no compiling on rented machines. All still correct.

### 3. Strengthenings

Each entry: the evidence, what to change and where, the expected effect, with the sceptics' amendments folded in.

#### 3.1 Define the solver's backup rule properly: Prob-max^n with a stated opponent model, a fixed tie order, and soft teacher targets (C04)

**Evidence.** PLAN 6.7 and 15 say "the policy head's preference decides ties; this is the Prob-max^n idea". That is a misreading. Prob-max^n (Sturtevant, Zinkevich & Bowling 2006) is an **expected-value backup** [at the other players' turns, average the outcomes weighted by how likely each choice is under a model of that player], not a tie-break. In 41 ties are pervasive, not rare as section 15 assumes: a leaf's value is the match-equity of the state reached, which depends only on which team won each trick, who holds the best trump, whether the 5 was played and whether a game ended, so many lines share one value. Sturtevant proved (thesis, Theorem 4.1) that with a mis-modelled tie rule the error of a max^n value is unbounded, and that "the tie-breaking rule must be implicitly defined by the way we order successors". In exhaustively searched 3-player Spades a wrong opponent model dropped a 74.7% win rate to 44.0% (soft-max^n paper).

**Change (6.7, 8 phase A, 13 M2, 15).** State the backup rule as part of the solver's definition. At the searching team's nodes, maximise its own money-chance entry. At the other three teams' nodes: before M4 (no network yet), a fixed, deterministic, position-only successor order (e.g. lowest equivalent card first) that never depends on search history; this also defines the tie rule under which the M2 identity "pruning on == pruning off" is asserted. From M4, a policy-weighted expectation with a small floor so every legal card has non-zero weight. Keep pure max^n with the fixed order as the brute-force reference. Document that phase A labels are exact *relative to this opponent model*, and train the policy head on the set of tied-best cards or the full action-value distribution, never one arbitrary tied card. Add an M2 test reporting the fraction of tied nodes and how often the root choice flips under a different tie order.

**Amendments.** Sturtevant's last-branch and speculative pruning are proved for max backups; once other teams' nodes use expectations, either re-prove which prunes remain sound (bounds on each component of a convex combination) or disable pruning in that mode and re-run the on/off identity. To control cost, restrict the policy weighting to the tied-best children under max^n (a soft tie-break) or a small top-k, and measure both against the fixed order at M4. Label post-mortem errors as "money given away against the modelled opponents".

**Effect.** A teacher whose values are robust to how the other teams actually play, smoother supervised labels, and an M2 gate that is well defined.

#### 3.2 Fix the main engine's shape: one recursion level, cheap inner player early and solver-based inner player late, determinised per-world UCT in the pool, single-tree ISMCTS and GO-MCTS demoted (C07)

**Evidence.** Both published recursive wins used one level with a solver-based PIMC player inside (Furtak's RK160,10; Bouzy's MC2-D-20-20 at +0.24, sigma 0.05). Bouzy's deeper levels: MC3-R +0.20 beat MC2-R +0.10, MC4-R +0.16 did not improve further ("the third recursion is actually not beneficial"), so depth 2 is a measured arm, not a curiosity. In open-hand Spades, determinised UCT [one search tree per sampled world, averaged across worlds] scored 245.91 vs 202.71 for max^n and let a moon-shooter through 250 times vs 1,377 of 3,244 clinchable hands (Sturtevant 2008); ensemble per-world trees beat a single shared tree in Doppelkopf (4.52 vs −1.25). GO-MCTS lost to Kermit by 9.84 points per game at about 42 s per move. EPIMC's gains are on Dark Chess, not card games. The plan's "Furtak's suggestion" **is** in the 2013 paper ("a hybrid player that uses recursive evaluations only in the late game"), so keep the attribution; note it rests on a post-mortem proxy.

**Change (6.8, 6.7 role 5, 2, 16, 18).** Fix recursion depth at one by default, depth two as an explicit experiment. Inner player at tricks 1–2: policy-network rollouts (the solver is unaffordable there, section 4.1). From trick 3, where the solver is nearly free: one-level recursion whose inner player is PIMC with the exact solver (Furtak's RK form), with plain exact-solver PIMC late only as a pool baseline, because plain PIMC reintroduces strategy fusion exactly where held-back top trumps and hidden burns resolve. Keep both hybrids ("PIMC early, recursive late" and "rollouts early, exact late") in the pool with the switch point set by SPRT in money. Add determinised per-world UCT with max^n backups to the pool as the candidate for close blocking decisions, labelled "open-hand UCT result composed with ensemble-UCT, unproven in 41". Rewrite the ISMCTS sentence as "no trick-taking result shows single-tree ISMCTS beating a solver- or rollout-based PIMC-class engine (Furtak & Buro 2013; Cowling et al. 2012 found no large difference in Dou Di Zhu)". In section 2 quote the thesis numbers (Hearts 8.22 vs 8.82; Spades 5.65 vs 5.68 "negligible") instead of 8.01/8.99 and 5.72/5.61.

**Effect.** The engine stops promising depth that buys nothing, keeps strategy-fusion-free play where it matters most, and gains a pool candidate aimed at the "deny the leader every trick" pattern.

#### 3.3 Test blocking as a joint outcome with a "leader can clinch" deal library (C09)

**Evidence.** "Every other team denies the leader any trick" is a joint outcome of three teams searching independently on different private information; each would rather someone else burn the top trump (a volunteer's dilemma), and which equilibrium self-play lands on is not fixed by any theorem (Devlin & Kudenko 2011: shaping "can alter the joint policy converged upon"). Sturtevant built his moon library from 10,000 games (3,244 clinchable hands) and found exact max^n let the shooter through 42% of the time.

**Change (14, 9, 15).** Build a library of "the leader could clinch this hand" deals from duplicate-seeded self-play at a fixed bot version, with "could clinch" decided by the open-hand solver or oracle head so the library is exact; regenerate it per generation. Replay each deal with all three blocking teams searching independently, report the leader's clinch rate with confidence intervals per engine and per tier, and repeat with one and two blocking seats played by the rules bot (stand-ins for human partners). Also report the blockers' own money change: under H7 a block that costs a blocker more money than it saves is an error, so the test must not reward blocking for its own sake. Optionally split the clinch rate by which team would have had to burn the top trump, to see free-riding directly. Add MP-Mix-style "offensive against the leader" and "paranoid when leading" bots as arena baselines and reference behaviours, never in the champion. Keep the existing single-bot scenarios as the fast unit-level check.

**Effect.** The one behaviour the owner cares about most becomes a measured property rather than an assertion.

#### 3.4 Inside a sampled world the oracle head is the cut-off evaluator; make truncated-vs-full rollouts and independent-vs-single-seat search mandatory M5 experiments at every tier (C11)

**Evidence.** If a truncated rollout is cut off with the seat-view value head, every world's leaf collapses to nearly the same number and the per-world money differences that drive blocking are averaged away. Learned Belief Search found full-length rollouts with a learned belief slightly worse than truncated ones bootstrapped by a value (24.59 vs 24.62 with the best blueprint) and two seats searching independently off one blueprint worse than one searcher (24.34 vs 24.48); SPARTA says independent search has "poor" empirical performance. Sturtevant & White 2006 is the precedent for a learned open-hand evaluator inside sampled search. 41 tables may have seven searching bots and 12–13 never-revealed cards, so both effects can be larger than in Hanabi.

**Change (6.5 head 4, 6.8, 6.10, 7.1, 13 M5).** Rewrite head 4 as "never sees the real hidden cards; inside a sampled world it may evaluate that hypothesis". Run the cut-off evaluator as a three-arm M5 comparison (oracle head on the sampled world; seat-view head; LBS-style sum of seat-view heads inside the world) rather than fixing it by decree, and train the oracle head on rollout-generated positions so it is calibrated where it is used. Make two M5 experiments mandatory at every tier, measured in money on the 14-way duplicate block (section 4.9): (a) truncated rollouts plus value bootstrap vs full-hand rollouts at equal budget; (b) all seven bots searching independently vs search for one seat per team or a tighter shared deviation threshold. Let SPRT, not the tier, decide where truncation is used. Extend the information-firewall test in 14 to assert the oracle head's inputs come only from the sampled world.

**Effect.** Possibly stronger play at lower cost on every tier, and early detection of partner-mismatch loss.

#### 3.5 Specify the money-head loss and calibration, and a sensitivity test on the right scale (C14)

**Evidence.** The plan names "log-odds accuracy" and "calibration" but no loss or metric. Cross-entropy on a four-way softmax is a strictly proper scoring rule [a loss whose minimiser is the true probability] and lives natively on the log-odds scale; temperature scaling (Guo et al. 2017) is the cheap proven calibration fix; equal-width calibration bins are unstable for rare probabilities that are never the top label (Nixon et al. 2019), which is exactly a far-behind team's regime. Zare's bound shows a match-equity table wrong by a few tenths of a percent costs almost nothing (100,000 GNU Backgammon matches: 50.1% vs 49.9%).

**Change (6.6, 9, 14, 15).** Four-way softmax heads trained with cross-entropy, distilled from the exact table at hand boundaries with soft targets; post-hoc temperature scaling on held-out matches; report log loss, Brier and adaptive-binned, all-label reliability with bins reaching 0.1–1%. Add a sensitivity test: perturb the table **in log-odds** (about ±5% relative on each chance, renormalised), not ±0.5% absolute, because an absolute 0.5% shock swamps a 0.1% chance and would declare every far-behind decision a tie by construction; a scenario whose decision flips is first investigated as a table defect, then declared a tie governed by the tie rule (3.1). Focal-style losses only as gated challengers.

**Effect.** Calibrated small chances where blocking decisions live, with standard tooling.

#### 3.6 State what governs a far-behind team's play below the search's resolution, and log that resolution (C15)

**Evidence.** A team's chance can be 0.001 while the search's noise floor is of order 0.01, so its decisions are then made by the deviation threshold and the blueprint's habit. SPARTA's threshold is 0.05 with a 100-rollout minimum. With common random numbers the relevant noise is the *paired* standard error of the best-vs-second margin (much smaller than a raw binomial estimate), and exact late-trick enumeration removes sampling noise entirely in the last tricks, so the floor bites mainly early.

**Change (5.3, 15, 6.8, 14).** Write that below resolution the deviation threshold keeps a team on the blueprint, which the self-play league and distillation have made (and which is therefore money-trained). Log the paired standard error of the margin per decision; add a per-tier diagnostic of how often far-behind decisions fall below it. Make the deviation threshold scale-aware (relative to the team's current equity, or in log-odds) so a 0.001 team can still deviate on gains that are large for it. **Do not relax section 14:** scenario expected choices are asserted exactly using the exact solver or enumeration at the scenario's depth, and the search at full T3 budget must reproduce them. Raise the owner question (section 7) only in the form "which money-maximising tie-break", never points or tricks.

#### 3.7 Match state and table features as explicit inputs to inference; belief quality measured per match-state class (C18)

**Evidence.** Policy-based inference weights each world by the product of the policy's probabilities for every observed play (Rebstock 2019, Eq. 1). A top trump thrown away to block a team on 36 is likely at that score and absurd at 0–0; a weighting policy blind to the match state misreads exactly the public-enemy plays. The shared network already carries the match state, so the real gaps are the table-derived features (section 4.4) and a per-class test suite.

**Change (6.4, 9, 14).** State that world weighting uses the same encoder as the blueprint, including match state and table features. Report Rebstock's true-state sampling ratio and true-world rate per match-state class defined by the table features themselves ("some team can clinch this hand", "decider game", "early match", "far-behind team"), and add hand-built score-driven plays to the belief suite with the expected posterior shift stated.

#### 3.8 Pre-register the M3 PIMC-suitability measurement (C22)

**Evidence.** Long et al. 2010 measured Skat and Hearts at leaf correlation 0.8–1.0 and disambiguation about 0.6 (10,000 games per type, 1,000 near-leaf samples), and found 15% of Skat games unresolved with three tricks left, where PIMC loses 0.42 points per deal. 41 keeps 12–13 cards hidden all hand, so its disambiguation will be lower. But their own Figure 3(b) shows PIMC's loss is *not* monotone in disambiguation (worst at mid-range), so a fixed numeric threshold would misread the paper.

**Change (13 M3, 5.1, 9).** Pre-register the procedure (10,000 hands; random playouts; near-leaf correlation/bias samples; information-set shrinkage per move, with the world-counting routine of section 4.5 optional), report it per trick including the fraction of hands unresolved at trick 4, and use it to prioritise which late-trick candidates get tournament time first. Drop the threshold-triggered default; the tournament decides, per 6.7/6.8.

#### 3.9 Make the belief head autoregressive with hard-constraint masking (C23)

**Evidence.** An independent per-card location head cannot be sampled into legal worlds without rejection (hand sizes, voids, trump limits, the robber holding the ace) and gives no exact probability for the world proposed, so the plan's own importance weight (6.4 item 3) is at best defined up to a high-variance constant. Solinas et al. could use marginals because Skat's 42-million-state information set can be enumerated; 41's cannot (1.5 × 10^36 worlds at trick 1). The proven belief for sampled search is autoregressive [assign hidden cards one at a time, each conditioned on the ones already placed]: Learned Belief Search (card-by-card, trained by summed negative log-likelihood), Off-Team Belief Learning, Ataraxos (transformer decoder; the dou dizhu belief net has 3.3M parameters, trained 8 hours on 4 H100s).

**Change (6.4, 6.5 head 3).** Encoder over the observation, then a decoder that assigns each unseen card in a fixed order to one of the locations (7 other hands, undealt pack, robber's burn, dealer's burns), masking locations that are full or forbidden by the tracker at every step; the proposal probability is the product of the conditionals. Add a per-step feasibility check (a matching/capacity test between remaining cards and open locations, or most-constrained cards first) with rejection as fallback, and log the rejection rate. Default generator for T2/T3; tournament challenger against the marginal-plus-masking generator on small tiers, judged by true-state sampling ratio, effective sample size of the importance weights, and money. Count the decode steps in each tier's budget and reuse particles across tricks so full decoding happens at trick 1 and on refills. Keep per-card marginals as a diagnostic.

#### 3.10 Train the belief head on a mixture of trajectories and monitor a valid-sample health metric (C24)

**Evidence.** A belief trained only on the champion's self-play is accurate only while everyone plays like that champion. In Hanabi, vanilla belief sampling produced a valid sample on only about 83% of time steps under partner shift, while off-team belief learning stayed above 99.5% and cut the self-play/cross-play gap below 1%. The plan never says what data the belief head trains on across generations, and tables will contain humans and old bot versions from launch.

**Change (6.4, 8 phase D, 8 phase E).** Each generation, train the belief head on a mixture of league trajectories (current champion, past versions, noisy and rule-based bots, later the human model), with per-source weights set by the cross-play tournaments; this is the Rebstock/Solinas precedent (inference networks trained on heterogeneous human pools) and costs nothing because phase B's league already produces the games. Add off-team-style reweighting (fictitious transitions unrolled with the champion) only if the measured valid-sample rate under partner shift shows a problem. With the masked autoregressive generator every sample is legal, so the health metrics are the effective sample size of the weights and the true-world sampling rate; monitor a 99% target and define a fallback ladder: autoregressive masked sampler → MCMC sampler (already in 6.4) → constraint-only uniform belief, logging which rung was used per decision. Keep the floor.

#### 3.11 Distil full per-card action-value distributions (soft targets) and store them (C25)

**Evidence.** Phases A, C and D all copy only the chosen card. Expert Iteration measured a network trained on the search's full distribution 50 ± 13 Elo stronger than one trained on the chosen action, with top-1 accuracy almost unchanged (the gain comes from cost-sensitivity, exactly what near-tie money values need). Hinton-style soft targets are the standard distillation.

**Change (8 phases A, C, D; 6.2; 12.7).** For every searched decision store the per-card money-chance 4-vectors and sample counts in the **training shards** (not the replay record, which stays action-only). Offer both policy targets to the tournament: the per-card rollout-count distribution (the ExIt analogue for a flat search) and a softmax of per-card values at a tuned temperature. Keep the money outcome / table value as the primary value-head target (ExIt, AlphaZero and KataGo all train value heads on outcomes); a blend with the search-root average is an SPRT-gated option, never a replacement. This also removes the tie-noise problem of 3.1.

#### 3.12 Specify the league: K-best past champions plus skill-level checkpoints, with a cycling detector (C26)

**Evidence.** The league is named but not specified, and a four-team general-sum self-play has no convergence guarantee (every RL-plus-search guarantee cited is two-player zero-sum). Eight copies of one network learn private conventions humans do not share. Fictitious Co-Play's ablation shows skill-level checkpoints are what make a bot a good partner to strangers (10.6 vs 4.7 with a human proxy; 8.6 vs 1.0 with random partners), architecture diversity adds nothing, the population plateaus at about 32, and 114 human participants preferred FCP partners. AlphaHoldem's K-best self-play is the opponent-pool precedent.

**Change (8 phase B, 8 phase E, 13 M9, 15).** Pool = K-best past champions by money rating, plus checkpoints along each run (initialisation, mid-strength, converged), plus rule-based and noisy bots; independent seeds added only when the M6 budget allows (FCP's 32-member population was 32 full runs). Sample partners and/or opponents from the pool for a tournament-decided fraction (start 10–30%). Reuse the pool as the phase-E cross-play suite and the M9 population. Cycling detector: a duplicate SPRT on money against the frozen pool plus the section-9 exploitability probe every generation; a generation that beats its predecessor but loses money against the pool is not promoted (the section-9 gate against the champion still applies). State that the FCP evidence is two-player cooperative and K-best is two-player zero-sum, so these knobs have no published values for four teams.

#### 3.13 Make the PPO bullet precise: oracle critic, stabilisers, and the right DMC challenger (C31)

**Evidence.** The plan's "PPO with the training-only oracle judge (PerfectDou; Suphx)" conflates two techniques. PerfectDou's oracle is a critic (perfect information into the value network only; γ = 1.0, GAE λ = 0.95, entropy 0.1, model lag 1). Suphx's "oracle guiding" trains the *policy* on perfect features and anneals them out, and Suphx reports plain distillation from an oracle policy "does not work well" and needs learning rate ÷10 plus rejection of large importance weights afterwards. OADMCDou (IJCAI 2024) found that inside Deep Monte-Carlo [learn each move's value directly from final results], fixed perfect features did not beat DouZero in 30 days while a linear anneal from 1 to 0 over 20 days plus clipped Q-updates did (ADP +0.104 ± 0.041).

**Change (8 phase B).** PPO with GAE, discount 1.0, a four-way softmax critic fed the oracle input, value-loss clipping (AlphaHoldem's Trinal-Clip), PerfectDou's settings as starting points to tune, target-entropy control and actor-learner lag as tunables. Challenger: "DMC + oracle feature dropout annealed 1 → 0 + Q-ratio clipping" (OADMCDou), not plain DouZero DMC. Never distil a policy from an all-cards-visible policy; the oracle input never reaches the policy head at execution.

#### 3.14 Residual MLP in the M4 pool at matched inference cost (C30, with the sceptics' correction)

**Evidence.** DouRN raised win rate against DouZero to 52–57% by stacking 2–6 residual blocks *on top of* DouZero's MLP, with 2–3× longer training; simple replacement at the same depth gave 50.3%. So "residual beats plain at equal cost" is not established. The plan's own rule is "the tournament keeps whichever plays better for the same compute".

**Change (6.5).** Add the residual MLP (2–6 blocks) to the M4 pool at matched FLOPs per decision; the plain MLP stays the default until SPRT says otherwise. No LSTM over history (the full history is already in the input).

#### 3.15 Phase E ordering and the interim human model (C33)

**Evidence.** Human-data methods need volumes a new app will not have soon: piKL's Hanabi imitation model used 235,954 online games; Bakhtin et al.'s no-press Diplomacy (arXiv 2210.05492) about 46,000 games (Cicero is a separate Science 2022 paper). Off-Belief Learning restricts itself to common-payoff games and is not in the plan. The plan's "simpler human-like play model until phase E" (6.4) and "simple rules bot" (9) are unspecified.

**Change (6.4, 8 phase E, 9, 13 M9).** Specify the rules bot built from the published 25/45-family heuristics checked against RULES.md (lead a low plain card; keep a saver; don't trump early unless the trick is worth it; hold the 5/J/A♥ for a late trick; count trumps; fight for trick 4; never strip partner's trumps; play against whoever is near the target) as the interim human-like predictor in belief weighting, with a small tournament-tuned mixture weight, never as a card chooser or strength target. Set a human-data threshold with the owner below which behaviour cloning and piKL are not attempted, triggered by a held-out test (the human model must beat the blueprint at predicting human plays). Keep OBL out. The rest of phase E's order is already right.

#### 3.16 Diagnostics-only auxiliary heads (C34)

**Evidence.** Auxiliary prediction targets never used to choose a card are a large proven source of sample efficiency (KataGo's ownership/score heads 1.65×, auxiliary policy target 1.30×; DouZero+'s hand prediction). The belief head is already one.

**Change (6.5, 5.3, 14).** Add "tricks per team this hand" and "game ends this hand" as cross-entropy heads with small loss weights, as an M6 ablation (dropped unless they beat the no-auxiliary baseline on SPRT). Drop the proposed "holder of the 5/Joker/A♥" head: it is a slice of the belief head. Enforce the scope at the type level (their outputs are not the money-chance type; no solver, search or arena code can consume them; excluded from exported student weights) and list them explicitly in the objective audit.

### 4. Simplifications and engineering savings

Each keeps answers identical or is gated so play cannot get worse.

#### 4.1 The exact solver is not cheap at trick 1: re-scope by trick index and measure before budgeting (C01, blocking)

Three independent enumerations of random 41 deals put a trick-1 open-hand world at roughly 10^12 raw leaf paths and about 7 × 10^8 node expansions even with a perfect trick-boundary transposition table [a memory of positions already solved] and equivalent cards (0.1–150 s per world depending on deal and seat, 3.5–15 million table entries), falling about 100× per trick (≈5 × 10^6 at trick 2, 5 × 10^4 at trick 3, 500 at trick 4). max^n admits only shallow pruning (Korf 1991: no average-case gain; Sturtevant 2003: +1–3 ply in card games), and the millisecond bridge/Skat solvers depend on two-sided zero-window alpha-beta [a win/loss yes-no search that a four-way money vector cannot use]. A 128 MB Cloudflare Worker cannot host a trick-1 solve.

**Change (5.1, 6.7, 7.1, 10, 12.3, 13 M2).** Say the solver is cheap *late in the hand*, not per world. Full-hand exact max^n is the default only from trick 3, for post-mortems, and as the M2 brute-force ground truth at every trick (however slow). At tricks 1–2 any PIMC use takes a small measured world count, or a depth-limited search cut off by the oracle head on the sampled world (reuses the solver). Extend the M2 gate to publish nodes, table hit rates, memory and milliseconds per world by trick index and seat on the 4-vCPU box and one cheap EPYC; replace "orders of magnitude" with measured factors; treat all the numbers above as placeholders until then. Keep the Setrograde set-based idea in 6.7 as the main lever for early-trick solves. **Quality unchanged:** the solver itself is untouched; only where it is applied and how its cost is measured change, and any cheaper substitute enters a tier only through the SPRT gate.

#### 4.2 Re-scope phase A: a short bootstrap, exact solves only from trick 3 (C02, blocking)

With 4.1's costs, exact PIMC at every decision is roughly 5–50 core-minutes per hand at 20 worlds, so "tens of millions" of hands is impossible on one rented machine. The pay-off is small anyway: at five cards a recursive player with a random inner simulator is on a par with exact-solver PIMC (Bouzy: +0.10, sigma 0.05), DouZero's from-scratch RL overtook a 50-million-sample supervised network within two days, and Expert Iteration started from 100,000 search moves.

**Change (8 phase A, 5.2, 12.4, 13 M3/M4).** About 10^5–2 × 10^6 teacher hands (or a stratified sample of decisions over match states and tricks that covers the late-match states that drive blocking: one game in, 31–40 points, mid-hand game ends), exact solves from trick 3, cheap sampled play earlier, per-decision action-value vectors recorded. Train to the held-out validation plateau and gate on SPRT (not "until it beats the rules bot"). Keep teacher-data generation as a background chunk type so the labelled set keeps growing at no extra cost. Belief-head training data does not need the teacher (true locations are known in any self-play), so generate it at scale with cheap bots. Start phase B with a KL penalty toward the teacher decaying to zero, and run one budget-capped from-scratch RL arm as a control at M6 under the SPRT gate. Re-size the range once M2 publishes cost per decision. **Quality unchanged:** phase A only seeds learning and is never the champion; its ceiling is PIMC's.

#### 4.3 The match-equity model as an exact backward-induction table built at M1 (C03)

Reachable per-team scores are only 15 values ({0,5,...,40} and {11,16,...,36}; 6 is unreachable because the first bonus trick scores 11); the bonus flag equals (score mod 5 == 1) and is derived; the dealer matters only through its team (partners are a symmetry of random deals). That gives 15^4 × 16 games-won patterns × 4 dealer teams = 3,240,000 states, about 810,000 under the four team rotations (about 13 MB as f32). Every hand adds at least 25 points to the table or ends a game, so the state graph is acyclic and one backward sweep is exact given each state's hand-outcome distribution. This is how backgammon tables have been built for 20 years (Rockwell-Kazaross: every score rolled out 38,880 times; XG2: 100,000, "piecemeal so that all possible resulting scores had already been rolled out, with the bot's table updated"). As written the plan is circular: the M2 solver and M3 teacher score lines with a model first built at M4.

**Change (6.6, 6.5, 6.7, 8 phases A/D, 10, 13 M1/M2/M4).** Replace "learned from self-play, cross-checked by DP" with the exact table: backward induction over (scores, games won, dealer team) with the sweep order "more games won first, then more total points", the three hand transitions spelled out (normal end: add points; game end: scores and flags reset, that team's games +1; match end: terminal 1/0 vector), hand-outcome distributions estimated by duplicate-seeded rollouts of bots that already use the finalised successor equities, iterated until the maximum change falls below a stated tolerance, with stratified rollout counts (more at states the arena actually reaches) and a learned smoother fitted on the log-odds scale for rarely reached states. Store in f32 (never f16 probabilities). Publish per-state sample sizes and standard errors; say "exact given the sampled hand-outcome distributions". Build v0 at M1 from the rules bot (used only by solver tests and the M3 tournament), refresh before the M4 teacher-data run from PIMC self-play with a small-chance calibration check, then per generation, logging the maximum change. Record the table version hash in every game record. Replace "dealer seat" with "dealer team" and derive the flag. Keep the eventual-match-result calibration check and the DP cross-check as standing tests. "Within-hand heads agree with the table at hand boundaries" becomes a test, not a mechanism. **Quality improves:** a regression of an exactly computable quantity only adds error where 5.3 says accuracy matters most.

#### 4.4 Train money heads toward the table value at the end of the current hand; replace fed-back head outputs with exact table features; separate public-input model (C13)

The eventual match result is an extremely noisy target for a hand early in a match; the table value at the hand boundary is the same expectation with far lower variance (Mortal's per-round reward = change in expected payoff with the true final placement at the last boundary; Suphx's per-round reward is the same construction). Feeding a head's own per-trick output back as an input (6.5/6.6) means two passes per evaluation, serving skew, and inside rollouts an unbudgeted network call per simulated trick per seat per world. A "public-only" head on a trunk whose inputs contain private cards cannot be guaranteed leak-free.

**Change (8 phase A, 6.6, 6.5).** (1) Value, oracle and public heads train toward the exact table value at the actual end of the current hand (a game or match ending mid-hand maps to its successor state); keep the eventual result as a held-out calibration check, and allow a TD(λ)-style blend as a gated option. (2) Replace the fed-back chances with deterministic features from the public state and the table in one lookup: each team's equity if the hand ended now, per-team "can clinch this hand" flags that model the jink, the 5-of-trumps immediate bonus, the deferred end-of-hand bonus and each team's first/later bonus flag, with "best/worst reachable successor equity" as a gated extra; define them over the engine's exact end checks in the Rust encoder. (3) The public money-chance head becomes a separate tiny sub-module with a public-only input slice (shipped in the same weights file), used for game-record timelines and diagnostics; whether its estimate is also fed as a feature is decided by SPRT. **Quality unchanged or better:** same objective, lower-variance targets, no serving skew.

#### 4.5 Replace "thousands of worlds" and "list every remaining world" with measured plateaus and a counted threshold; describe the sampler once (C08)

From one seat 41 has about 1.5 × 10^36 consistent worlds at trick 1, 6.6 × 10^15 at trick 4 (the last real decision) and 2.5 × 10^8 even at trick 5, against Skat's 4.3 × 10^7 at the start of play, so "exact late enumeration" essentially never triggers. Published plateaus: 20 worlds at five cards for solver-based PIMC and about 600 for rollout-based (Bouzy), 160 in Skat (Furtak), with recursion continuing to improve with hardware; Doppelkopf's 5/2000, 10/1000 and 20/500 splits were within error bars; SPARTA's 100-rollout minimum then dropping options more than two standard deviations behind cut rollouts about 10×.

**Change (6.8, 6.4, 7.1, 10, 13 M3).** Set worlds per tier from a measured decision-agreement curve, with the literature plateaus as lower starting points (T3 may exceed them while money still rises under SPRT); shared world pool reused for every candidate card; the 2-SD skip only after the 100-rollout floor, under common random numbers. Replace enumeration-by-trick with a counted threshold: a dynamic program over the suit-length matrix counts constrained worlds (honouring voids, trump limits and the burn constraints of 5.2) and enumerates only below a tier budget (e.g. 10^4); treat trick 5 as decision-free. Describe the belief sampler once as a resample-move particle filter [weights updated by policy likelihood, resampling when the effective sample size falls below N/2, History Filtering swap moves for rejuvenation]. Drop the unverified Ataraxos budget rule. **Quality unchanged:** same estimator, budgets measured instead of assumed.

#### 4.6 Keep power-equivalent cards with 41-specific exceptions; move partition search off the critical path (C06)

Partition search's published gains (GIB's b → b^0.76; Furtak's 22–58×) come from alpha-beta proof trees; in max^n every node is an "all" node and no paper applies partition search to max^n or vector values. Only the relabelling symmetry transfers (measured 3.5–76× fewer raw paths, 1.5–5× fewer boundary states). Furtak's rank-only definition is unsafe in 41: the 5 and J are adjacent yet the 5's bonus scores at once (rule 29) while the J's waits (rule 30); J, Joker and A♥ force different sets (rule 17); only the A♥ may be held back against the ace of trumps (rule 16); with hearts as trumps there is no separate ace of trumps.

**Change (6.7, 6.8, 2, 10, 13).** Define equivalence per hand by exact rule semantics (identical legal-move consequences, forcing and holdback rights, bonus timing, adjacency in the effective order, counting the current trick-winning card as outstanding), as a relabelling that maps every legal line to a legal line with the same trick winners, bonus events and game ends. Add positive test decks (red 10 vs 9, black 2 vs 3, plain K vs Q with nothing between: must collapse) and negative ones (5 vs J; J vs Joker; Joker vs A♥; A♥ vs ace of trumps), and a randomised M2 test comparing vectors with and without collapsing. Record partition search as deferred with its own exactness gate; keep Kermit's near-equal-card merging and ProbCut out (both change answers). Remove partition search from crates/solver in 13 until measured.

#### 4.7 Transposition-table key carries the in-hand score state, keyed at trick boundaries, same move order everywhere (C05)

Under the money objective the value is not additive in points: it depends on tricks won per team this hand, whether the 5 has gone, the best trump so far and its team, and bonus flags, because mid-hand game ends depend on them. Skat solvers drop the running score only because theirs is a scalar additive score; a score-free key in 41 would silently merge positions with different money values. Edelkamp keys only after a completed trick; Sturtevant's thesis requires the same move order in every part of the tree.

**Change (6.7, 10, 13 M2, 14).** Key = (remaining hands, leader, tricks per team this hand, 5-played, best trump so far and its team, per-team bonus flags); start-of-hand scores, games won and dealer team are constants per solve. Position-only successor order (3.1) that survives speculative-pruning re-search. Tests: positions differing only in in-hand score state never share a value; pruned vs unpruned vs brute force on tie-heavy positions; hit rates and memory per world by trick. Stored values are the exact table values; if a compact key is needed, quantise only the key, never the value vector. Expect smaller transposition gains than bridge (Sturtevant 2004: 157× in Spades but 26× in Hearts, for the same reason).

#### 4.8 Model the burn as a hidden action (C10)

Under H2 nothing happens between the burn and the first card, so evaluating "the burn" separately searches the card choice inside anyway and costs the same worlds twice. Policy-based inference treats every action as evidence; a burn is an action whose identity is hidden but whose probability given a world is computable, so a world must name the burned card(s) (Kermit's defenders likewise enumerate the soloist's hidden discards).

**Change (6.9, 6.7, 6.8, 6.4, 6.3).** The robber's decision is the joint (burn, first card) action searched once over the shared worlds (up to 5 × 5 combinations), still recorded and shown as two actions; valid only while H2 holds. The robber's burn node sits at that seat's first turn in trick 1 and the dealer's forced burns before the first lead; in sampled worlds, seats that have not yet robbed get a burn branch so worlds stay consistent (a world in which the dealer took a turned Joker then a turned ace carries two dealer burns). Belief locations: 7 hands, undealt pack, robber's burn, dealer's burn(s), with exact sizes (robber's burn exactly one card and never the ace; dealer 0–2 with the count public; the robber's post-rob hand exactly 5 of its 6). The burn probability comes from the same policy-head burn output used for play, and enters each world's weight. Test that every sampled world satisfies the burn constraints.

#### 4.9 Tournament statistics: multi-team rating, corrected AIVAT figure, the 14-way block as the unit of analysis, defined championship format (C16)

Elo is a two-player model with no published form for a one-winner-three-losers table. Every published multi-player card-game evaluation replays each deal under every seat assignment and treats that block as the observation (Sturtevant: 14 arrangements for 4 players; GO-MCTS 2024: "14-way tournament ... every seating permutation except all A or all B", Wilcoxon signed-rank over blocks), the multi-player analogue of Fishtest's game-pair rule. On AIVAT the plan's citation is actually right: Burch et al. AAAI 2018 report an 85% standard-deviation cut (44× fewer games) in the DeepStack man-machine match, where one player's strategy was fully known; bot-vs-bot the same paper reports about 3× in standard deviation and roughly 10× fewer hands, and MIVAT in six-player poker gave only 20–25%. So "often 10–40×" is unsupported for 41.

**Change (9, 10, 13 crates/arena, 17).** Rating of record: an offline Plackett-Luce model [a standard model for ranking several competitors at once, whose output is literally a predicted chance of winning the table] fitted by maximum likelihood over all tables; online dashboards: OpenSkill's Plackett-Luce or TrueSkill with ranks [1,2,2,2] and team skill = sum of seat skills at mixed tables; Elo only as a diagnostic for two-program reports. Rewrite the AIVAT line as above with "41's figure to be measured at M1"; implement both AIVAT terms in bot-only tournaments (all strategies are known): the per-hand chance control variate using the oracle head with a baseline averaged over fresh shuffles of the same state, and the known-strategy action term; store values in the record and verify the null (mean correction ≈ 0). Define the candidate-vs-incumbent block as the 14-way block (every A/B assignment to the four team slots except AAAA and BBBB), with the deck for hand k derived from (block, k) so rotations keep sharing cards after matches diverge; run the generalised SPRT on block scores with Fishtest-style bounds and a two-stage gate that is **reject-only at the cheap stage** (promotion requires passing at the full T3 budget). Championship: knockout = the 14-way block; final = one four-way table replayed under all 24 team-to-slot permutations; size M1 arena chunks accordingly.

#### 4.10 Counter-based common random numbers and asynchronous batching (C21)

Common random numbers reduce variance only if each draw keeps the same role across the candidate cards compared; a shared sequential stream desynchronises as soon as two candidates lead to different trick sequences. Lock-step batching idles cores because rollouts end at different tricks (games end mid-hand).

**Change (6.8, 10, 12.4).** Derive the noise for (decision, world, seat, card-index-within-that-seat's-remaining-hand) from SplitMix64 of those indices with the project RNG; form network batches from whichever rollouts are ready, over-provisioning 2–3× the thread count (EnvPool's pattern). Define tournament and replay budgets in worlds/rollouts, not wall time, so decisions are byte-identical regardless of completion order; require inference kernels to give per-row results independent of batch size and order (add that test beside the SIMD-vs-reference check). **Quality unchanged:** same estimator, less noise, fuller cores.

#### 4.11 Network sizes, a measured throughput number, decisions per generation, and a budget line (C27)

The plan names no network size, latency target, actor throughput or decisions per generation, so the M4/M6 gates and rental budget cannot be sized. Reference points: DouZero 48 cores + four 1080Ti, passing DeltaDou at about 5 × 10^9 steps (30 days total); PerfectDou 880 cores + 8 GPUs, 2.5 × 10^9 steps; DanZero+ PPO under a day only because it started from a 30-day DMC model; Suphx 44 GPUs × 2 days per agent. 41 is at the cheap end (≤5 actions, ~42 decisions per hand, 8 seat-examples per hand).

**Change (6.5, 7.1, 8, 12.4, 13 M4/M6).** Add a sizing table as the initial tournament pool (teacher: residual/plain MLP 4–6 blocks of width 512, 2–4M parameters; T2 student 2–3 × 256; T0/T1 student 2 × 128–256) and a budget line, **every figure labelled an estimate to be replaced by measurement**: phase B generation ≈ 10^9–10^10 decisions, 1–2 days on a 64-core, 4 × RTX 3090 box; first full pass ≈ 6–10 weeks of machine time at roughly €1,000–2,000. The M4 gate becomes "measure and publish decisions per second per core for engine plus actor inference on a cheap EPYC/Xeon, and derive the cost of one phase-B generation from it before any GPU rental" (not a fixed 2,000–5,000 pass/fail), feeding the launcher's value-per-dollar ranking. A generation ends when its SPRT gate says so, never when a budget line runs out.

#### 4.12 Budget phase C and add KataGo's playout-cap randomisation (C28)

At the T3 budget as written (thousands of worlds, every seat finishing the hand) one decision is about 175,000 policy evaluations, so a 64-core box records only thousands of search hands per day, and 12.4's "distillation run" has no budget. KataGo measured playout-cap randomisation [full search and recording on about 25% of decisions, fast search elsewhere] at 1.37× training efficiency at equal strength; SPARTA's UCB plus deviation threshold gives about 10×.

**Change (8 phase C, 12.4, 6.8, 13 M7).** Explicit generator flags: rollouts per decision (from the M5 strength-versus-rollouts curve) and fraction of full-search decisions (A/B at M7: 100% vs 25% at equal compute, judged by the next generation's SPRT); record targets only from full-search decisions. Estimated 200–1,000 rollouts per decision, about 1 core-minute per recorded hand, 100–200k hands per day on 64 cores, one million-hand generation in 3–7 days. Forced playouts with policy-target pruning are PUCT tree mechanics and apply only to the MCTS variant in the 6.8 pool. This budget applies to data generation only, never to the live T3 bot.

#### 4.13 NNUE-style integer inference with an incremental first-layer accumulator, as a gated student candidate (C29)

The plan's MLP path is float-only and gives no mechanism for "incremental evaluation along a rollout" (10). Float through matrixmultiply cannot be bit-identical between x86 (FMA kernels) and WASM simd128 (no FMA). Stockfish NNUE [a shallow network evaluated millions of times per second in integers, with the first layer updated incrementally as features change] reports negligible quantisation error for shallow nets and "the incremental implementation is consistent". 41's sparse one-hot encoder, where a card played changes a handful of features, is exactly NNUE's structure. No card-game int8 strength study exists, hence the gate.

**Change (6.5, 7.1, 8 phase D, 10, 11.1, 13).** Design the Rust encoder now (M4) as a list of active feature indices, since it serves both paths; continuous inputs (scores, money chances) need bucketed one-hots or a small dense side-path. After M5, add quantisation-aware training for students (weights clamped as NNUE does), int16/int8 kernels with int32 accumulation and saturating clipped ReLU behind multiversion (AVX2, AVX-512-VNNI, simd128, plus a non-AVX2 integer path for v2 builds), fixed-point softmax for rollout sampling, and the incremental accumulator copied when branching across candidate cards. Teacher and T3 stay f32; f32 remains the exact reference. Gate integer students against their f32 twins by SPRT at equal wall-clock per tier (so extra worlds count); the M4 gate stays "within tolerance" for float and gains "bit-identical native vs WASM" only once the integer path exists. Expected 2–4× faster rollouts and 4× smaller weights on T0/T1.

#### 4.14 Defer the transformer challenger, ONNX, tract, ruzstd, arrow/parquet and mimalloc; drop ort (C32)

#### 4.15 Determinism tiers: a fixed-point money-chance type and index-ordered reductions (C35)

The plan promises byte-identical chunk re-runs and a kill-the-machine test with identical results (14) while summing float money chances over worlds under rayon's work-stealing (its docs: float sums "are not fully deterministic") and dispatching between FMA and non-FMA kernels. Fixed-point integer sums are associative, so results are identical across thread counts, CPU classes, Windows/Linux and WASM; NNUE does integers inside evaluation. **Change (14, 12.7, 6.7–6.8, 13 M4, 11.1; apply at M0 when the type is born):** money chances are u32 in units of 2^-32 per team, normalised to sum to 2^32 by largest-remainder rounding (four u32 cannot sum to 2^32 directly, so hold the sum in u64), u64 accumulators for sums over worlds, world weights converted to fixed point at the network boundary and every later multiply-add in integers; rayon maps each (world, candidate) into a pre-sized slot and a sequential reduce follows. Floats stay only inside the network forward pass, reproducible per (binary, CPU class). Scope 14 accordingly (engine, solver and teacher data identical everywhere; network-dependent data identical per CPU class; the kill-the-machine test pins the replacement to the same binary and CPU class or uses integer inference). Tests: fixed-point solver matches an f64 reference solver's card choices on random positions; identical results across 1, 4 and 64 threads, native and WASM. Resolution 2^-32 ≈ 2 × 10^-10 is far finer than any decision needs, so no coarse rounding is involved (see section 6).

#### 4.16 Write the Cloudflare limits into the design (C36)

Verified: 128 MB per isolate including WebAssembly allocations; global scope must finish within 1 s; 64 MiB uncompressed script with no compressed limit; CPU 30 s by default and up to 5 min on Paid; Durable Object hibernation resets in-memory state and per-socket attachments are capped at 16,384 bytes. **Change (6.2, 7.1, 7.2, 13 M8):** the Durable Object persists table state (seed, action log, scores, table version) in storage after every action and reconstructs by replay; the T1 Worker loads weights lazily on first decision and caps worlds, particles and tables by a memory budget measured on the real Worker; no WASM threads; the M1 table-manager design is already storage-backed and replayable so M8 is a thin port; the memory budget is a per-tier knob next to network size and search budget; keep "fan-out" as one sentence in 15 as an unbuilt fallback.

#### 4.17 Serverless T2: do not build the fan-out now; Containers are the leading T2 candidate, decided at M8 (C37, amended)

#### 4.20 Lint policy for SIMD kernels (C40)

`forbid(unsafe_code)` cannot be overridden lower down. ### 6. Considered and rejected

- **C20, round money chances inside the solver to enable Sturtevant's discrete cut-off and a two-sided zero-window search.** Refuted unanimously. With chances summing to 1 and any step fine enough to respect 6.6's small-chance accuracy (10^-4 or finer), the discrete cut-off prunes when the bounds sum exceeds 1 − 2 × 10^-4, i.e. nothing beyond the continuous cut-off; a step coarse enough to matter (about 1%) would erase the small differences that blocking and kingmaking depend on. The forced-win / forced-denial yes-no query needs no rounding at all; it survives only as a test-only, diagnostics-only brute-force check ("can the other three teams deny team A every remaining trick in this world / in all consistent worlds") for the section-14 scenarios, never as a decision rule or a second solver engine.
- **Parts struck from surviving changes:** labelling "Furtak's suggestion" unverified (the passage exists); plain exact-solver PIMC as the late-hand default (reintroduces strategy fusion; RK-style recursion with the solver inside is the default, plain PIMC a baseline); a fixed numeric disambiguation threshold that would switch engines automatically (Long et al.'s curve is non-monotone; the tournament decides); relaxing section 14's scenario assertions for far-behind teams (assert exactly with the exact solver); a 2,000–5,000 decisions/s/core pass/fail gate and dollar/calendar caps (measure, don't cap); the residual MLP as default on every tier (pool candidate at matched cost); forced playouts with policy-target pruning in the main engine (MCTS-only); a ±0.5% absolute table perturbation (use log-odds); the "holder of the top trumps" auxiliary head (redundant with the belief head); mandating three independent seeds per generation for the league (cost); f16 probability storage for the table; a GHCR fine-grained per-package token (GHCR only takes classic tokens); Containers fixed as the T2 default before measurement; naive "train the belief on mixed data" described as Off-Team Belief Learning (it is a different procedure; mixture training is the sufficient, precedented form).

### 7. Questions only the owner can settle

**A. Money and match rules (these decide whether "chance of the match" is the whole objective)**
1. Does anything change the amount paid: a jink doubling the stake, a 2-0 match paying more than 2-1, a losing team paying less for finishing second? H6 says every loser pays the same; if any such rule exists, the objective must be an expected-money vector, not a win chance, and the type in 5.3 is insufficient.
2. Do matches always run to completion? If a match can be cut short (pub closing, a player leaving) and settled by games or points, that termination rule must be part of the table's state.
3. With four teams a match can run to five games. Is there any cap on games or hands, and does "first to two games" ever change?
4. "Reach 41" means 41 or more (a team on 40 taking a plain trick, or on 36 taking the 11-point trick, wins)? Our default: yes.
5. Team A on 36 wins trick 5 with a plain card (41) while team B on 36 holds the highest trump from an earlier trick with +6 pending. Who wins? Our default: the trick scores first, so A.
6. A pending end-of-hand bonus is simply lost when a game ends at an earlier trick by a plain trick? Our default: yes.

9. The first dealer is drawn once per match and the deal then moves one seat left after every hand, across games and abandoned hands, never re-drawn? And the engine's fixed deal (two then three, starting left of the dealer) is acceptable under rule 4?

# Part 2: completeness critic

**Completeness critic: what the final report still misses (most important first)**

1. **The M3 teacher has no early-trick evaluator.** Report 3.2/4.1 say tricks 1–2 use "policy-network rollouts" or "a depth-limited search cut off by the oracle head", but at M3 no network exists (M4 builds it). The only proven stand-ins are rollout PIMC with random or rules-bot inner players [finish each guessed deal with a cheap simulated player] (Bouzy MC-R, +0.10 at five cards; Furtak's RK rollouts) or exact PIMC on a small measured world count. **Do:** name one of those as the M3 default for tricks 1–2 and move "cut off by the oracle head" to M5.

2. **3.6's "scale-aware" deviation threshold is an invention and contradicts its own argument.** A relative or log-odds threshold is in no cited paper, and for a 0.001-equity team the paired standard error (≈0.003 at 100 rollouts) is bigger than the equity, so a relative threshold would deviate on noise. **Do:** keep SPARTA's form: deviate only when the margin exceeds k standard errors of the paired difference plus an absolute floor; the far-behind case is then handled by the floor, not by scaling.

3. **"Full T3 budget" is undefined, so the promotion gate (4.9) is undefined.** 7.1's "thousands of worlds" was struck (4.5) and the real budget waits on owner Q12. Also PLAN 14 promises SPRT error rates fixed in advance, but the report sets no α, β or effect size in stake units, and never costs a 14-way block (14 full matches, 2–5 games each) at T3. **Do:** fix T3 in worlds/rollouts per decision (4.10) before M5, pre-register α=β and the H1 margin per gate, and publish block cost per tier.

4. **The "solver-based inner player from trick 3" is T2/T3-only, but the report does not say so.** Outer worlds × candidates × ~24 remaining decisions × inner worlds × ~5×10^4 nodes is ~10^9 node expansions per decision: fine on a server, impossible in 1 CPU-second on a phone or Worker. **Do:** state per tier which inner player applies; T0/T1 use policy rollouts throughout.

5. **Determinism scope omits GPU training.** PLAN 11.2 picks FP32/FP16/BF16 by rented GPU generation, so trained weights are not reproducible across machines; 4.15's kill-the-machine test "identical final results" cannot hold for RL/distillation runs. **Do:** scope the identity test to data-generation chunks; training resumes from checkpoints with logged divergence; define "CPU class" as the multiversion feature level actually dispatched and record it in every record.

Activity: research branch commits from ≥16 May 2026, main merges 25 Jul and 22–23 Aug 2026; nine branches, all last touched Jul–Aug 2026.

**Exact open-hand solver** (`examples/fortyfives_dds.py`, fast port `examples/fortyfives_dds_fast.py`):
- Depth-first **fail-soft alpha-beta minimax**; NS seats maximise, EW minimise — each partnership is one player. **Not max^n** (only two sides exist).
- Payoff modes: `delta` (NS game-point change after bid adjustment: failed bid = −bid value, made 30-bid = flat 60), `raw` (NS−EW raw points), `net` (ΔNS−ΔEW). The 100+ pegging rule is not modelled (asserted).
- Transposition table keyed by `(hands, trick, leader, ns_tricks, best_rank, best_par)` with exact/lower/upper flags; cleared when the trick count changes. Fast port: int64 hand bitmasks, trick packed 7 bits/seat, TT key = four hand masks + packed state ("order-insensitive"), entry packed into one int64.
- Equivalent cards: pools = trump suit + A♥, and each non-trump suit minus A♥; two cards in a pool with no live card strictly between them form one move class; A♥ never merged; a trump pair straddling the current highest-trump rank is not merged (the +5 bonus distinguishes them). Representatives searched by descending rank.
- Two opponent models: `minimax` (classic double dummy) and `rulebased` (EW forced to the rule bot's choice; docstring warns "Paranoid minimax is NOT an upper bound there").
- Timings (PR #39): ≈2.7 ms vs 71 ms per fresh 5-card-deal solve (≈25×); an n=500 evaluation chunk 64 s vs 40.5 min (≈38×). No node counts published.
Sources: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jitousig/fortyfives-ai/main/examples/fortyfives_dds.py , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jitousig/fortyfives-ai/main/examples/fortyfives_dds_fast.py , https://github.com/jitousig/fortyfives-ai/pull/39

**Solver validation gate** (`tests/test_dds_vs_engine.py`, four layers): (1) engine parity over 60 random play-outs — legal moves == engine `get_legal_plays`, trick winners, running high trump, final NS/EW deltas vs engine `end_hand` and an independent leaf re-implementation; (2) search vs brute-force minimax on 80 random endgames (40×1-card, 30×2-card, 10×3-card) in both opponent modes × three payoffs; `root_values` must aggregate to `solve`; move reduction on/off must give identical root values on 12 full deals; 25 partial-trick cases; (3) rule-based fidelity: 40 seeds, >500 decisions, `rb_fallbacks` must be 0; (4) engine-grounded best response: 30 seeds (≥16 must run), exhaustive search over deep-copied real engine states after two tricks. Fast solver: bit-identical `root_values` on 70 random deals, every decision.
Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jitousig/fortyfives-ai/main/tests/test_dds_vs_engine.py

**Evaluation "canaries" and discipline.** `evaluate_paired`: same seed per hand in two environments; the candidate controls both NS seats' card play, everything else is rule-based; per-hand diff = candidate points − baseline points; CI = ±1.96·sd/√n. **Canary:** rule bot vs itself must give exactly +0.0000 and 0.0% wins (checked up to 500 hands; run aborts otherwise); re-run after any engine/eval edit; a separate bidding canary also must be +0.0000. Yardstick: random-legal ≈ −0.50, rule-based = 0.00 (bidding "net" yardstick: random ≈ −28). Standing rules: confirm wins at n≥2000 on ≥2 independent seeds (0 and 100000) with CI excluding 0; one change per commit; "never trust small-N, single-seed, or Elo-ladder results". No "capped/near-optimal" claim allowed without a perfect-information oracle upper bound (decision gate).
Sources: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jitousig/fortyfives-ai/main/examples/play_eval.py , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jitousig/fortyfives-ai/main/examples/oracle_gap.py , RESEARCH.md

**PIMC design.** PIMC v3 (`fortyfives_pimc.py`): `n_worlds=20`; worlds dealt from unseen cards with hand sizes (5−tricks played, −1 if already played to the trick), **hard void inference** (a seat that plays neither lead suit nor trump is void in the lead suit), 8 retries then unconstrained fallback; playout = rule-based strategy with approximate legality; score = our points − their points; best mean wins. v1 = unconstrained + cheap playout, v2 = + void inference, v3 = + rule-based playout. PIMC-DDS keeps the v3 sampler, replaces the playout by an exact per-world solve, averages in **delta (final-payoff) space** "because E[delta] ≠ f(E[raw]) at make-or-set decisions", equal world weights (constraints are hard filters, no likelihood weighting).
Sources: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jitousig/fortyfives-ai/main/examples/fortyfives_pimc.py , https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jitousig/fortyfives-ai/main/examples/fortyfives_pimc_dds.py

**"Discard-count-constrained determinization" (lever 1).** Rule-based discarders keep only trumps, so a seat that drew `rc` replacement cards holds 5−rc trumps; `min_trumps = max(0, (5−rc) − trumps already played)`; sampler deals each seat at least that many trumps, then fills; 8 retries. Described as "near-exact vs rule-based discarders; a heuristic prior vs humans". Constraint present on 100% of determinize calls and binding on 55% (30 real hands). **Lever 2** (auction-conditioned): replay the public bidding; accept a world only if every hidden seat's reconstructed pre-discard hand reproduces its bids/passes (≤64 tries; ≈96.5% accepted, ≈8 tries/world, ≈3.5% fallback).

### 7. Int8/int16 quantised inference for game networks — **partly verified; no card-game measurement exists**

- **Stockfish NNUE docs** (verified, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/master/docs/nnue.md): feature transformer int16 weights/biases/accumulator; hidden layers int8 weights and int8 inputs with int32 accumulation; ClippedReLU outputs int8 (0..127). "Quantization inevitably introduces error that accumulates more the deeper the network is", but for shallow NNUE nets it is negligible; floats "sacrifice too much speed for too little accuracy gains"; int8 inputs obtained "without losing too much precision"; weights must be **clamped during training** ("modify the optimizer to clamp the values to the available range after each optimization step"); clipping threat features costs "negligible loss in evaluation quality". **No Elo figure is published.**
- **Lc0** (verified quotes): 2018-08-23 blog — "We tried to quantize existing nets, but it doesn't really work that way. Elo drop was about −300" (post-training int8 of a large CNN; https://lczero.org/blog/2018/08/test10-learning-rate-has-been-lowered/); 2024-02-28 — "finally having success with INT8 quantization, which could speed up our models by 50% without quality degradation" (claim, policy-only per forum discussion; https://lczero.org/blog/2024/02/transformer-progress/); 2026 GSoC page still lists int8/fp8 as not fully evaluated. 2018 forum reports fp16 "does not reduce playing strength significantly" (**likely**, anecdotal).
- **KataGo**: ships FP16 by default on CUDA/ROCm/OpenCL/ANE with FP32 fallbacks (verified in `gtp_example.cfg`); no int8 in the engine; PR #1231 (Aug 2026, closed unmerged) adds experimental INT8/FP8 ONNX PTQ tooling and "makes no portable speed or Elo claim" (https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/pull/1231).
- DouZero, Mortal/Mjai, Suphx: **no published quantisation measurements found** (searched; uncertain).
Takeaway: the only documented safe recipe for small MLPs is NNUE's int16-then-int8 with range clamping during training; post-training int8 of an untrained-for-it large net cost −300 Elo.

### (b) Points for the owner to settle

1. **Joker rank.** The app never states it and changed it in v1.0.1. Irish 31/41 players put it 3rd, between the Jack and the Ace of Hearts, as RULES.md does. Can you open the app's 41 rules/tutorial screen and confirm the Joker sits between the J and the A♥ (and not below the A♥, as one secondary site claims)?
2. **Best-trump value.** The app says a flat "+6"; Irish players say a flat "11 rather than 10". Nothing online supports "6 the first time, 5 every later time in the same game" (rule 28). Is that your table's rule, and should the engine also be able to run a flat +6 for app-style play?
3. **When the bonus counts.** The app is silent. Rule 29/30 (the 5's trick counts at once; any other best trump only at the end of the hand) agrees with the 2016 forum ruling on a 41 dispute. Confirm: if the 5 has not appeared but the Jack or Joker has been played, the bonus cannot be claimed before the hand ends?
4. **Robbing compulsory or optional?** The app (and all traditional sources) make robbing a choice for the ace-holder; RULES.md makes it compulsory with a penalty. Confirm compulsory. (Related: under the app's wording you discard first and then take the turned card, so you cannot burn the card you take; H3 allows it. With compulsory robbing, burning the taken card is the only way to decline it — is that intended?)
5. **Turned Joker.** The app documents nothing. Two traditions exist: dealer takes it, burns, and the next card is turned (rule 24; also older 45 texts) versus the Cork practice where the dealer names the trump suit. Confirm rule 24, including the forced second rob/burn when the new card is an ace or the dealer holds the ace of trumps.
6. **Jink and score reset.** The app documents no jink. One Irish 45 variant lets a five-trick hand win the game but carries the other pairs' points into the next game; rule 36 resets everyone to nought. Confirm the full reset after a jink and after a normal 41.
7. **Which trumps may be reneged.** RULES.md: 5, J, Joker, A♥. The app (for 25) lists 5, J, A♥ and says nothing about the Joker in 41; some Irish 45 tables allow only the 5. Confirm the Joker reneges like the others (held back against A♥ or lower, forced out by the J or the 5).
8. **Does any highest trump earn the bonus?** Rule 30 gives it even to a low trump; one Irish 45 variant gives it only when the best trump played is one of the top four. Confirm "any highest trump".
9. **Table size.** The app runs 2–10 players with pairs or threes; the 2015 forum 41 game had 6 players in 3 pairs. RULES.md fixes 8 players in 4 pairs. Is 8-handed the only configuration the bots need?
10. **Match format.** The app's "best of 3" matches H6's first-to-two-games; it has no stakes. Nothing to change, but note the app cannot be used as a reference for money settlement.
